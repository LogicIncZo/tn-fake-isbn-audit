The Anatomy of a Fake Book — three records, taken apart

TL;DR

They did not write a book. They assembled one.

The ledger proves the ISBNs are stolen. This page proves something more damning: the books themselves are assembled from other people's work. We took the catalog records apart line by line and matched every ingredient against the open web — OpenLibrary, archive.org, publisher pages, Google Books. The catalog's own summary paragraphs are lifted verbatim from real books by real publishers. The titles are remixes of real titles and subtitles. The authors are either nobody (no trace in any catalog, anywhere) or somebody — a real, notable person who never wrote the book under their name.

Three specimens below, each carrying two physical copies bought with public money on 13 April 2024, now shelved in the Reference section on the third floor of the Anna Centenary Library, Chennai. Every quoted string on this page can be checked by pasting it into a search box.

The Recipe

Five ingredients, one fake

Ingredient 1 · The blurbCopy the publisher's summary of a real book, word for word, into the library catalog record. No rewriting — verbatim is easier and reads professional. The result: the fake book's only prose belongs to somebody else's book.
Ingredient 2 · The titleRemix a phrase from the real book's subtitle or chapter list into a new title. Close enough to look scholarly; different enough to dodge a title search.
Ingredient 3 · The authorInvent a name that sounds plausible for the subject — or borrow a real, notable name in the same field. In both cases the named person has no work matching the book.
Ingredient 4 · The imprintPrint "Phoenix, 2023" with a rotating country of publication ([USA], Germany, India) — impossible for one publisher, and consistent with the Coimbatore allegations.
Ingredient 5 · The ISBNPaste an ISBN that actually belongs to an unrelated book — in all three specimens below, a Hindi textbook registered to a legitimate registrant years before the fake was "published".
Specimen 1 · ACL #526241

"An Era of Divorce" — a fake whose summary names the real book

The catalog record — Anna Centenary Library, Chennai
TitleAn Era of Divorce
AuthorAinsworth, Wittig
ImprintPhoenix 2023 [USA] · 257p. 23 cm · English
ISBN978-93-9236-004-6 — registry assigns it to Conceptual Framework of Education-1 (Vivek Malviya, Hindi, registered 23/09/2021)
Acquisition2 copies · accessions 677068, 677069 · 13.04.2024 · Reference, 3rd floor

The catalog's summary, quoted in full:

"The widening gap between the rich and the poor is turning the American dream into an impossibility for many, particularly children and families. And as the children of low-income families grow to adulthood, they have less access to opportunities and resources than their higher-income peers—and increasing odds of repeating the experiences of their parents. Families in an Era of Increasing Inequality probes the complex relations between social inequality and child development and examines possibilities for disrupting these ongoing patterns."

— the fake record's summary, pasted word-for-word. The bolded sentence names the book it was stolen from: Families in an Era of Increasing Inequality: Diverging Destinies (Amato, Booth, McHale & Van Hook, eds., Springer, 2015). The cataloger did not even delete the real title.

The title — "An Era of Divorce" is a phrase-fragment of the real book's subtitle world (Families in an Era of…). No book with this exact title exists on OpenLibrary, Google Books or the web. The author — "Wittig Ainsworth" is no traceable author anywhere; the surname pair points at two real figures in the very subject the DDC class number (306.89, divorce) claims: Monique Wittig (novelist-theorist) and Mary Ainsworth (attachment psychologist). Two notables' names, zero of their work. Status: blurb lift confirmed (byte-identical strings); author mash inferred — no person of that name exists, both referents are real.

Specimen 2 · ACL #526291

"Democracy and Peoples Right in The Contemporary World" — the real editor becomes the fake author

The catalog record — Anna Centenary Library, Chennai
TitleDemocracy and Peoples Right in The Contemporary World
AuthorDreze, Jean
ImprintPhoenix 2023 [USA] · English
ISBN978-93-9236-009-1 — registry assigns it to Kaaryaalayee Hindee Aur Kampyootar ("Office Hindi and Computer", Vivek Malviya, Hindi, registered 15/02/2022)
Acquisition2 copies · accessions 677168, 677169 · 13.04.2024 · Reference, 3rd floor

The catalog's summary, quoted in full:

"Chomsky's early insights into the workings of power in the modern world remain timely and compelling. Published for the first time, this series of lectures also provides the reader with an invaluable introduction to the essential ideas of one of the leading thinkers of our time"

— verbatim from the publisher page of Democracy and Power: The Delhi Lectures, Noam Chomsky with Jean Drèze (Open Book Publishers, 2014).

This is the cruelest of the three. The fake book borrows the name of Jean Drèze — the development economist, long-time co-author of Amartya Sen — who is not some random famous name here: he is the real editor and interviewer of the very book the blurb was stolen from. The title is a mash of that book's chapter headings ("The Vicissitudes of Democracy", "Militarism, Democracy and People's Right to Information", "The Nationality Question in the Contemporary World"). The summary still says "Chomsky's". The actual object on the shelf is none of it. Status: blurb lift and chapter-mash confirmed (strings match the OBP page); "Drèze never wrote this" confirmed by absence from his bibliography.

A book about "The Single Woman™" — trademark in the blurb, God-centered and Twitter-fans and all — shelved as the work of a nonexistent "Jane Naomi" under a stolen Hindi-textbook ISBN, in a government library's Reference section. An exact-title real book does exist (A Single Woman, Jeanmarie Simpson Bishop, 2016 — the Jeannette Rankin play), and is unrelated to everything else in this record. Status: blurb lift confirmed; author fabrication confirmed by absence (no traceable "Jane Naomi" author, only unrelated namesakes).

The Scale

Three specimens are not outliers — they are the method

On 07–08 November 2026 we ran a fabrication screen over 293 records of the original 569-record wave ledger (pre-full-catalog expansion): 270 Kalaignar Centenary Library (Madurai) records by author identity, and the 23-record Anna Centenary Library deep-sample by title, author and blurb. Every check used open catalogs and the open web; every evidence URL is in the ledger.

Among the 70 borrowed names: B. R. Ambedkar appears as the author of 19 different Madurai records — including a "20 volumes of Writings and Speeches" set logged as separate fake entries — and the blurb-lifting habit extends across the sample (one record's summary still advertises Noam Chomsky; another lifts a 2019 open-access chapter's abstract). The full row-by-row verdicts, with evidence URLs for each, are in the ledger (fabrication columns) and the repo data files.

Honest limits: the screen classifies catalog records, not physical objects — the shelf-inspection, print-quality and bill-chain questions are for DVAC, which holds the complaint. "No trace" means no record in OpenLibrary, WorldCat, Google Books or the open web, as of the screen date; it cannot prove a self-published book nobody ever cataloged, which is why specimen verdicts also rest on the positive matches (the verbatim blurbs, the registry originals).

The Ghosts Speak · பேய் ஆசிரியர்கள்

What would the authors have said, if any of them had existed?

One hundred and fifty records in the screen carry an author who traces to no publication, no biography, no person — names minted for a copyright page. The five below are drawn from the screen's actual verdicts. Everything factual around each voice — biblio number, title, ISBN, shelf — is from the ledger. The voices are fiction, written by this desk: that is the point. A ghost is the only narrator a ghostwritten book honestly has.

Ledger ghost № 1 · ACL #526241 · KCL #182152 · cooked-from-existing-work

“Wittig Ainsworth” — An Era of Divorce (Phoenix 2023 [USA])

I am two real women glued at the comma. One of them built the science of how infants say hello; the other wrote books about women who refuse the sentences men write for them. Neither wrote a page about divorce. My summary is my alibi and my confession both: paste it into a search engine and it flies home to a Springer volume with four real editors. I am what remains when you delete the authors from a blurb and keep the blurb.
Ledger ghost № 2 · ACL #526287, #526288 · no-trace author

“Wiley Kefeng” — Language Politics and Language Contact; Reality-Testing of Existence and Character

My surname is a publishing house. My given name belongs to a real mathematician in California who has never heard of me. I wrote two books on two unrelated subjects in the same month, which is the only honest thing about my CV: nothing connects me to either. The chapter that loaned me my first title was written by two real scholars at Open Book Publishers — I am the bit left over when you remove the citation.
Ledger ghost № 3 · ACL #526299 · no-trace author, blurb lifted

“Naomi Jane” — A Single Woman (Phoenix c2023)

My book begins with another woman's name, her blog, her followers, even her trademark sign — the cataloger forgot to delete the advertisement. I never existed, so I cannot be embarrassed; but if shame were transferable, mine would arrive by the sentence. A reader opening me would meet a life I never lived, described with an enthusiasm I was never capable of.
Ledger ghost № 4 · KCL #100023 · no-trace author

“Henrick Björn” — Python Programming For Beginners Henrick Björn

Read my title slowly. I am on the cover, and I am also in the title, because the person who assembled me could not decide which kind of decoration I was. There are dozens of us on that shelf — Rasmus Lennart, Ansgar Rosmund, Filip Sandberg — one per book, Scandinavian to the ear, empty to the last page. Ask us what a variable is. We were never told.
Ledger ghost № 5 · KCL · no-trace author, surname-stitched

“Grothendieck DeWitt” — Homeomorphisms of the Mandelbrot Set

I was built the way these books are: take two surnames that have earned respect, fuse them with a space, attach a title from a field neither man worked in. One half of me redrew the foundations of geometry; the other half formulated gravity as path integrals. Neither half ever touched a Mandelbrot set. I am not a scholar. I am a credential wearing a tuxedo stitched from other people's reputations.

Fictional testimony, clearly so. These five names come from the fabrication screen's no-trace verdicts — no real person answers to them, which is why they can speak here without defaming anyone. The ghosts who were instead real people — Ambedkar on nineteen Madurai records, Drèze in a Chennai record, the real writer whose voice survives inside a stranger's summary — get no invented quotes, because their names were taken, not minted. Their evidence speaks for them: see the three specimens above.

Verify

Check any specimen yourself, in one minute

  • Open the live OPAC record: biblionumber 526241 · 526291 · 526299.
  • Copy the record's Summary. Paste it in quotes into any search engine — you will land on the real publisher's page (Springer, Open Book Publishers, Thomas Nelson).
  • Search the title on OpenLibrary and WorldCat — nothing with this exact title and author exists.
  • Check the ISBN at isbn.gov.in — each number belongs to a named Hindi textbook by a named registrant, years before "Phoenix 2023".

If your verification disagrees with ours, that is a finding, not a failure — open an issue with the biblionumber and what you saw. Corrections land in the repo with a dated re-test.
